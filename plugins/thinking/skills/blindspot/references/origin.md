# Origin and evidence limits

## Article contribution

Read [Felix Rieseberg's article](https://felixrieseberg.com/how-to-be-more-ambitious-a-tutorial-for-humans-with-models/)
in full on 2026-10-15, including on revision. Its framing covers speed, altitude,
depth, and range. This skill draws on these specific suggestions:

- Connect a requested task to the outcome it serves; reframing can change the work.
- Use professional methods, tools, and raw material rather than expert vocabulary.
- Challenge assumptions against underlying evidence and look for missing signals.
- Use outside validation rather than the model's own judgment alone.
- Describe activities to find useful perspectives from other fields; use their
  techniques rather than roleplaying their practitioners.

The article discusses everyday business work as well as creative examples. It
does not prescribe product discovery, discipline maps, source cards, named-person
quotas, prototype deliverables, or a fixed research ceremony. It also advocates
extreme goals and describes capability anecdotes; this skill does not treat those
as evidence that a claim is correct or a task is feasible. Its advice is workflow
inspiration, not a controlled evaluation of this skill.

## Local additions and observations

Evidence attribution, transfer limits, proportional effort, stopping conditions,
reuse checks, and respecting the current task's owner are local operational
choices. Do not attribute those detailed rules to the article.

The first draft, craft-discovery, was too product/experience oriented. Its toy,
CSV, and repair-event development runs produced useful distinctions: authored
behavior versus unnecessary architecture; diagnosing failures before accepting
an interface explanation; and a manual handoff instead of a new app. They also
exposed budget and outcome-proxy weaknesses. These are observations about a few
agent-produced answers, not evidence of product outcomes or proof of which
instruction caused the improvement.

User feedback prompted rewriting the core as a general approach-improvement pass,
not merely adding technical examples. Subsequent MLOps, software-design, and UX
runs remain in the originating repository's development records, outside this
portable skill. Evaluate
whether their decisions or methods improved, not whether they repeat headings.
No baseline/held-out adoption study or real operational/user outcome is implied.

## 2026-10-30 research and precedent refinement

The original Dots SpecLab skill was inspected at its introduction commit
`10aad12ac64534e7589846590bf4ae8f8bda6ed7`, along with
[PR 222](https://github.com/alex-furrier/dots/pull/222). The current tracked
SpecLab file is unchanged from that original version; no distinct older archived
predecessor was found in the scoped history. Its precedent research, derived
invariants and “What good means” sections informed the compact exemplar-to-criteria
connection here: inspect concrete strengths, transferable patterns, tradeoffs and
limits before adapting them. Its fixed exemplar counts, scored matrices, artifact
bundle and validation gauntlet are not part of Blindspot. The introduction records
integration rationale, not demonstrated skill effectiveness.

Actual external lookup for consequential expertise gaps, focused repository/PR
rationale and follow-up research, qualified outcome evidence, owner discovery
without outreach, and reconciliation of conflicting evidence reflect the user's
later clarification and local operational choices. They are not claims about the
Felix article or proof that a merged precedent succeeded in operation. Evaluation
continues outside the runtime skill; keep the earlier tie and later exploratory
results distinct from adoption or real user outcomes.
